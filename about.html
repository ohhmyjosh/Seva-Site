Seva / About

Why Seva exists.

India’s informal workforce is enormous and largely invisible to the tools that organise the formal economy. Seva is an attempt to fix one specific, solvable piece of that — in one city, properly, before anywhere else.

The problem

Both sides are missing the same thing: a trusted way to see and choose local help.

A daily-wage labourer waits at a chowk hoping someone hires him. A skilled maid’s income is capped by how many neighbours happen to have her number. Neither lacks ability. What they lack is a way to be found.

On the other side, households carry real anxiety about who they let into their home. Word-of-mouth already works in Varanasi — it just breaks the moment your usual person is unavailable, and it offers no way at all to judge a stranger.

Seva’s job is to make find someone reliable, right now, near me a five-second action instead of a round of phone calls. Done well, that is not only a business. It raises the floor of dignity and income for people the digital economy usually skips.

Mission

Make local, informal work visible, trusted, and fairly paid — starting with the people who need it most.

Vision

A city where anyone with a skill can be found, chosen, and paid with dignity — and anyone who needs help can find it in seconds.

Core values

Six commitments that decide the arguments.

Values are only real if they cost something. Each of these has already ruled out a feature, a shortcut, or a growth number that would otherwise have been tempting.

01

Dignity first

A worker is a person, not a listing. The product and the language around it treat them that way — including the words on this website.

02

Earned trust, never bought

Ratings and reliability decide who sits highest on the map. Money never buys rank, and there will never be a sponsored slot.

03

Local-first

Depth in one neighbourhood beats a thin presence across a city. Density is the strategy, not a stepping stone to a national launch.

04

Simple enough for anyone

A worker with a basic phone and little literacy should still be able to participate. If they cannot, the design has failed, not the worker.

05

Honest by default

The product never claims a protection it does not actually provide. If a feature is not built, the interface does not imply that it is.

06

Fairness above faith

Seva is inspired by values of service. Access to work is never conditioned on religion — and that boundary is written down so it cannot drift.

Non-negotiables

Five rules that need a constitutional argument to change.

Most product decisions are reversible and should be made quickly. These five are not. They are recorded here so that any future version of Seva — or any partner considering one — knows exactly what is not on the table.

01

No fee to exist on the platform

No registration fee, no subscription, no pay-to-rank. Ever. A worker who cannot afford ₹100 today is precisely the worker Seva is for.

02

Verification before public visibility

A worker is never shown to the public until an admin approves them. Fast signup is a promise to workers; gated visibility is a promise to households. Both are kept.

03

The worker controls their own terms

Rate, hours, and travel radius belong to the person doing the work. A pricing algorithm may advise them; it never decides for them.

04

Faith never gates work

No worker is ever required to attend any religious activity to get or keep jobs. No meeting is a condition of participation. The app serves everyone equally.

05

Never lie in the interface

The app only promises protections that exist. When a planned feature was found to be described as if it were already live, the words were changed — not the expectations.

A boundary worth stating

Business and ministry stay separate.

Seva is inspired by values of dignity, service, and honesty. That inspiration shapes how the company treats people. It never shapes who is allowed to work.

The hard rule

No worker is ever required to attend any religious activity to get or keep jobs. No seminar or meeting is a condition of participation. Any faith-related gathering is entirely optional and separate from the platform. The app serves everyone fairly, regardless of religion.

This is both a values commitment and a practical safeguard. Tying business access to faith would be discriminatory, would undermine trust across a diverse city, and would cap the market. Community networks may help Seva reach workers and customers — but community is a distribution channel, never a gate on who may work.

Who is behind it

A solo founder in Varanasi, building in the open.

Seva is a founder-mode project built by one engineer-founder with AI-assisted development. The operating philosophy is brutal practicality over polish: prove the model with real bookings before building the machinery of a big company.

The guiding line for scaling is deliberately uncomfortable — build systems that scale, don’t become the system. Everything in the roadmap is measured against it.
