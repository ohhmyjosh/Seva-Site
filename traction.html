Seva / Roadmap

Early, and specific about it.

Seva has a working app, a real codebase, a clear model, and first bookings in two categories. It does not yet have a validated business model or a repeatable growth engine. That is normal at this stage, and it is stated openly so nobody mistakes ambition for evidence.

Where we are

Traction, stated without inflation.

Download counts would be the wrong measure at this stage and we are not going to invent them. These are things that are true today and that a partner can verify.

Built The product

Onboarding, verification, live map, booking, OTP-confirmed completion and rating run end to end, in English and Hindi.

2 Categories with real bookings

Electricians and physiotherapy — the first bookings to run end to end, which is what proved the loop closes. The pilot now opens on salon work, elder care, maids and labour, where the weekly demand actually sits.

39 Service categories

A complete bilingual taxonomy in the product, not a list on a slide.

15 Localities mapped

Varanasi neighbourhoods with coordinates in the app, so a worker without GPS can still be placed.

The honest read

Almost everything below the product layer is still an assumption. Whether customers will trust a verified stranger in their home, whether workers will accept a commission, whether availability and ratings can beat word-of-mouth — none of these are proven. The pilot exists to convert them into evidence, one at a time.

Horizons

One year, three years, ten years.

Each is a condition to meet, not a date to hit — and each is tagged with how much evidence currently stands behind it.

In progress ~1 year

A dense pocket of Varanasi where real customers repeatedly book verified workers. Five to ten completed bookings a day, and proven reliability in at least a few categories.

Still a bet ~3 years

Varanasi-wide liquidity across most core categories, a commission mechanism that actually collects, and a playbook repeatable enough to hand to a second city.

The vision ~10 years

A trusted local-work platform across many Indian cities, where any skilled person can be visible and earn with dignity — and anyone who needs help can find it in seconds.

The plan

Five phases, in the order the flywheel actually jams.

This is a working roadmap rather than a wish list: each phase names what gets built, why that comes before the rest, and the single condition that ends it. Phases exit on evidence, not on dates — a date only tells you when we stopped, a condition tells you whether it worked. Nothing in a later phase begins while an earlier condition is unmet.

01 Now · in progress

Turn on verified identity

Everything downstream depends on the app knowing who someone really is. Until a worker signs in on their own handset, every trust guarantee rests on a typed phone number — workable for a demo, not for letting a stranger into a home. This phase is the last piece of engineering before the ground work starts.

  • SMS sign-in — a one-time code to a real handset, and the server takes identity from that session alone.
  • Named administrators — approval rights held by specific people rather than a shared password.
  • Audit trail — every approval, suspension and edit recorded against the person who made it.
  • Worker binding — each existing worker claims their own row by signing in, one handset at a time.
Ends when

Ten workers have signed in on their own handsets and are bound to their own profiles, with no profile claimed by the wrong person.

02 Next

Seed supply at the labour chowk

Supply comes first because demand for a marketplace with nobody on it is wasted. Labour is the opening category for one practical reason: those workers already gather in one place every morning, so onboarding costs a morning rather than a campaign. Household help and elder care are signed in the same pocket, through the same conversations.

  • Field onboarding, hardened — ID capture, locality entry and the Hindi wording fixed until the form never fails on a cheap handset.
  • Locality mapping — a worker without GPS still gets placed correctly from a named neighbourhood.
  • Verification queue — approvals turned around same-day, because a worker who waits a week does not come back.
Ends when

Twenty verified workers stand on the map inside one Varanasi pocket, concentrated in labour and household help rather than spread thin across thirty-nine categories.

03 Then

Open the four demand categories

Demand goes onto the same streets the supply came from — not city-wide, not online. The four categories households already ask for weekly are the only ones promoted: salon work, elder care, maids and labour. Everything else stays live in the product but unadvertised, so the first experience is never a search that returns nobody.

  • QR posters in the pocket — on the doors of the households whose streets the workers were signed on.
  • Realtime booking alerts — a worker who misses requests is a worker who leaves.
  • Category-first entry — the app opens on the four, so a first-time user lands somewhere staffed.
Ends when

Supply and demand are present in the same locality in the same week, and a household has placed a request without anyone from Seva standing next to them.

04 The proof

Turn the wheel ten times

Ten real completed bookings, confirmed by OTP and rated. Matched by hand where the product cannot do it yet — the point of this phase is evidence that the loop closes, not that it closes automatically. Repeat bookings matter more than new ones here, which is why maids and elder care carry this phase.

  • Completion OTP in the field — proven on real handsets in real homes, not in a test.
  • Ratings prompt — asked at the moment the job ends, when the answer is honest.
  • Repeat booking — rebook the same person in two taps, because that is the whole household-help relationship.
Ends when

A graph of completed bookings climbs in one locality, with at least some customers booking a second time — the only genuinely persuasive artefact this company can produce.

05 The decision

Measure, then widen

Four numbers decide whether the model travels: bookings per day, worker retention, customer repeat rate, average transaction value. Commission starts collecting here, against completed jobs only. If the numbers do not hold, the answer is to fix this pocket — not to open a second one and average the two together until the problem disappears.

  • Metrics dashboard — the four numbers, visible without anyone having to ask for them.
  • Commission collection — charged on completed jobs, never on signup, listing or rank.
  • The second pocket — the same playbook, run again, by someone who is not the founder.
Ends when

The four numbers hold for a full month in one pocket, and the playbook has been run a second time without the founder in the room.

The discipline behind this order

The biggest risk to a project like this is not competition. It is the temptation to build the artefacts of a big company — org charts, pricing formulas, pitch decks — instead of doing the one small unglamorous thing that proves the company should exist. Ten real bookings in one Varanasi locality is that thing.

Beyond the pilot

What gets built once the wheel turns.

Named here so it is clear these are deferred by choice rather than forgotten. Nothing on this list should be built before the pilot answers its questions.

Soon after

In-app payments

The only credible path to collecting commission, and therefore to Seva being a business rather than a service. Deducted at source, once bookings are proven.

Soon after

Dispute & refund flows

A real appeals process for suspended workers, refund handling, and an incident-response runbook. Today the founder handles these directly.

Soon after

A metrics dashboard

The four numbers that decide everything, visible daily rather than reconstructed monthly.

Later

Masked calling

A telephony provider behind the existing call-session structure, so neither side has to hand over a personal number.

Later

Domain Representatives

The first category operations lead, hired only when worker volume justifies the cost and revenue can sustain it.

Later

Background checks & insurance

Deeper verification and cover for home visits. Correct to want; wrong to promise before it exists.

The ask

Four things that would move Seva fastest, right now.

Named and specific, in rough order of impact. We would rather ask for four concrete things than for general support.

01

Field onboarding support

People who can help register workers at labour areas: documents, explanation in Hindi, and the trust that comes from a familiar face. This is the single highest-leverage help anyone can give.

02

CSR & sponsorship

Fund the pilot: onboarding drives, QR posters, awareness in one locality, and the operational cost of supervising a first rollout properly.

03

Local institutions

An introduction from an organisation a neighbourhood already trusts is worth more than any launch campaign — within the boundary that participation is never conditioned on faith.

04

Technical partnership

Realtime alerts, a payment integration, telephony credentials, and analytics. The gaps between a working product and a pilot-ready one.

On money, plainly

Around ₹30,000 has come in from early supporters so far. Company registration is deliberately deferred — spend goes to validation, not overhead.

That is enough to prove a locality. It is not enough to launch a company, and we are not pretending otherwise. The pilot ask is ₹10–15 lakh with a stated use of funds, and the right time to raise it is after the flywheel has turned a few times — not before.

Until then, the most useful thing anyone can offer is help getting the next ten bookings.
