Seva / Product

It is built. Here is exactly what it does.

Seva is not a deck with a mockup attached. The app runs, the database enforces the rules, and the admin console approves real workers. This page is the technical account — including the parts that are deliberately not finished.

The stack

Boring technology, deliberately.

Every choice here was made for a small team maintaining a live product in a place where a support call is a person walking to your door. Nothing exotic, nothing that needs a specialist to keep alive.

React + Vite
A mobile-first single-page app. Fast builds, instant reload, and a static bundle any host can serve.
Leaflet
The live worker map. Open, lightweight, and free of per-view billing surprises at exactly the moment a pilot starts working.
Supabase (PostgreSQL)
Workers, bookings and call sessions in Postgres. Registration, booking creation and every admin action run through server-side functions rather than client writes.
Capacitor
The same codebase ships as an Android app with location, camera and gallery permissions declared. Geolocation and uploads use standard web APIs, so iOS is a build step rather than a rewrite.
A services layer that owns the database
Pages and components on top, one services layer underneath that owns every database call. It also detects schema drift — unknown columns and legacy statuses — and adapts, so an evolving database never hard-fails in a worker’s hand.
Deployed on Vercel
SPA routing and asset caching configured, so reloading a deep link works instead of 404-ing.
Verification

A worker’s status is the whole safety model.

Every worker sits in exactly one of four states, and that state alone decides whether a household can see them. There is no second list, no override, and no way to be halfway visible.

Pending

Submitted, not visible

Where every self-registered worker starts. The application is complete and waiting for a person to read it.

Approved

On the map

Approval sets the verified flag too. Both are required together before a worker appears to anyone.

Rejected

Not listed

The application did not pass review. Verification is cleared and the profile stays off the public map.

Suspended

Removed

An approved worker can be taken down immediately. Trust that cannot be withdrawn is not trust, it is a badge.

The rule

Approved and verified, or invisible.

A worker appears on the public map only when both conditions hold. If a profile is missing exact coordinates the app falls back to locality coordinates rather than dropping the person entirely — being roughly placed is better than being erased.

Badges never overstate

Verification badges are independent checks, and only the ones that passed are shown. There is deliberately no failed or pending state: a plumber has no education to verify, and rendering “Education not verified” would make an ordinary profile look deficient for a question nobody asked of them.

Badges are derived from the underlying records, so one can never claim more than the evidence behind it.

Onboarding

Three profiles, because one form for everyone insults somebody.

Asking a maid for a graduation year is nonsense. Letting an electrician skip employment history defeats the point of verification. So the category a worker picks decides which profile they get.

General

Maid, labour, general helper

The lightest set. Identity, locality, rate and availability — nothing that would stall a registration for no safety gain.

Skilled

Electrician, plumber, carpenter, mason, repairs

Adds employment history and references — the checks that matter for someone doing paid work inside a home.

Professional

Physiotherapist, counsellor, tutor

Adds qualifications and certificates, because that is what a customer is actually buying in these categories.

Portfolio means different things to different trades

A painter’s photos sell the job. A tutor’s certificates do. A counsellor may want to show their clinic. A general helper has nothing to photograph — and asking them for images only makes onboarding longer and their profile look emptier. So the category decides the label, the prompt, and whether the step is offered at all.

Recent work
Up to six photos, for trades customers pick with their eyes.
Certificates
Up to four, for teaching and training categories.
Clinic or office
Optional, up to three — so a client knows what to expect.
None
Offered to nobody who has nothing to show. An empty gallery is worse than no gallery.
Inclusion

A platform for informal workers that only works on a smartphone is not a platform for informal workers.

The map needs GPS, and GPS needs a smartphone. That excludes exactly the workers who most need visibility — so keypad-phone workers are handled as a first-class case, not an edge case.

An admin registers them by hand, records a fixed locality, and saves their number. They are shown on the map by locality rather than live GPS, and they receive calls directly. No app required, and no live movement tracking implied that does not exist.

Why it is designed this way

Manual onboarding by an admin is a first-class path in the product, because many of the workers Seva exists for are not tech-savvy. If the field team has to sign someone up on paper at a labour chowk and enter it later, that is a supported flow — not a workaround.

A worker records the languages they can be hired in — Hindi, Bhojpuri, English, Urdu, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Nepali, ordered by how common they actually are around Varanasi. There is deliberately no “other” option: a free-text language nobody can filter on helps no customer choose.

Details that only matter if you have met the user

Small decisions, made the hard way.

A platform for informal workers fails on small things: a form that resets, a language that is not yours, a calendar you cannot keep. These are the ones already handled.

01

Availability stays coarse

Days of the week plus morning, afternoon, evening. A real-time calendar is a promise a worker on a feature phone cannot keep, and a booking that fails on a stale calendar damages trust more than “Mon–Sat, mornings” ever would.

02

No completion percentage for customers

Profile completion is shown to the worker and the admin, never to a customer. A “60% complete” badge tells a customer “this person is half-done” and suppresses bookings — a growth mechanic that becomes a trust penalty.

03

Typed locality is never overwritten

Live location fills the locality field when it can, but once a worker starts editing it by hand the app stops correcting them. Small detail; it is the difference between a form that helps and a form that argues.

04

Overlapping pins fan out

Density is the goal, so the map has to survive it. Workers stacked on the same street separate on tap rather than hiding each other.

05

Rate floors in two places

₹100 an hour and ₹300 a day, enforced in onboarding and again in the admin console, so nobody can be listed below the floor by either route.

06

Location failure is not a dead end

Two-stage GPS with caching, and a manual locality search if permission is denied. Saying no to location tracking should not end the session.

Data & privacy

The database is the security boundary, not the screen.

Filtering the map to approved workers in the app is a convenience. The real protection has to live in the database, because anything the app can request, anyone holding the app’s public key can request too.

That principle drove a round of work to move every privileged operation server-side and to close off what the public key can read.

Phone numbers
Not readable by the client. A booking derives the worker’s number server-side, for that booking only.
Government ID images
Held outside the public storage bucket. They exist for verification, not for browsing.
Registration & bookings
Created through server-side functions rather than direct client writes.
Admin actions
Approve, reject, suspend and trust level all run server-side against a fixed allow-list, authenticated separately from the app.
Trust-critical decisions
Verification approvals, dispute outcomes, suspensions and anything touching money stay human-owned. AI assists the work; it does not make those calls.
Honest status

What is live, and what is deliberately not.

Never lying in the interface is a non-negotiable, and it applies to this page too. Some of what follows is deferred on purpose — building payments before bookings are proven would be a way of avoiding the harder question.

Live

Registration, verification, map, booking, admin

Worker onboarding writes to Postgres, the admin console approves and suspends through guarded server-side functions, and only approved and verified workers reach the public map. This loop runs today, in English and Hindi.

By design

Cash payment, not in-app

The app records the booking amount; the money changes hands in cash. Online payment is deliberately deferred until the booking flow is proven with real jobs. Overbuilding payments first would be building the machinery of a company that has not yet earned one.

By design

The OTP lives in the app, not in an SMS

The code is generated and shown to the customer, and the worker enters it on site. SMS delivery costs money per message and adds nothing at pilot scale; it is a later upgrade, not a missing piece.

Deferred

Masked calling

Call sessions are recorded and the provider integration points are in place, but no telephony provider is connected — so calls go direct. The button says “Call worker” rather than implying a masking layer that does not exist yet. When it does exist, the wording will change with it.

Next

Realtime booking alerts and iOS

Workers currently see requests when they open the app. Realtime alerts are the highest-value remaining product gap. iOS is a build step, not a second codebase — the app is browser-first and Capacitor-friendly throughout.

Technical partnership

The deferred items are where a partner moves the needle fastest.

Telephony credentials, a payment integration, or engineering time on realtime alerts and reliability would move Seva from a working product to a pilot-ready one faster than anything else we could buy.
