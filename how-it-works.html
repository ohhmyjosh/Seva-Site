Seva / How it works

Two people. One job.

Everything on this page exists to make one moment trustworthy: the point where a household and a worker who have never met agree to a job. Seva is a two-sided market, and its hardest problem was never building features — it is getting both sides to show up in the same place at the same time.

The two journeys

Two separate paths that must meet at a single point.

Both sides progress independently until they converge at the job. If either path breaks before that meeting point, no value is created — and no revenue is possible. Every safety and money system exists to make that convergence trustworthy.

The household

Wants reliable help nearby, fast, and someone they can trust in their home.

  1. Open Seva. The map is the home screen — no search form to fill in first.
  2. Filter by service, or just look at who is actually nearby right now.
  3. Tap a pin. Photo, rating, distance, rate, languages, availability, badges.
  4. Book, or call the worker directly.
  5. Share the OTP when they arrive. That starts the job.
  6. Pay the agreed total in cash, then rate the work.

The worker

Wants steady jobs, income, visibility, and to be treated as a person rather than a listing.

  1. Register free in under a minute. No fee, no subscription, ever.
  2. Set your own rate, your hours, and how far you will travel.
  3. Upload a photo and a government ID. Wait for admin review.
  4. Once approved, you appear on the map to your whole neighbourhood.
  5. Accept a request, arrive, enter the customer’s OTP.
  6. Get paid, keep the rating — and get found more easily next time.
The trust flywheel

Trust is the engine, and ratings are the only throttle.

Seva compounds through one loop. A verified worker goes live, a customer finds and books them, the job completes and is confirmed by OTP, the customer rates it, and a good rating pushes the worker higher on the map — which brings more bookings and more income.

01Verified & liveAn admin approves the worker. Only then do they exist publicly.
02Found & bookedA nearby customer picks them from the map, price agreed first.
03Job done, OTP confirmedProof the work actually happened, on site, with both people there.
04RatedFeedback attaches to the worker and to that specific job.
05Higher on the mapGood work becomes more visible work. Money never buys this.
06The neighbours hearA good experience brings the customer back and pulls in the street.
Why this ring is the roadmap

Break any stage and the wheel stops turning. That is why work is prioritised by which stage is weakest rather than by which feature is most interesting — a beautiful new screen at stage four is worth nothing if stage one is jammed.

Trust & safety

Because workers enter homes, this is the most important system in Seva.

Safety begins with a hard rule: a worker is never visible to the public until an admin approves them. Signup is fast; visibility is earned through review.

01

Layers, not a single check

Phone verification, an ID upload, a profile photo, admin approval, booking records, an OTP-confirmed completion, ratings, and a complaint path that can pause or remove a worker. No single one of these is sufficient; together they are a reasonable answer to “who is this person at my door?”

02

Trust is not a one-time event

Approval gets a worker onto the map. Ratings and complaints keep deciding whether they stay there. Better ratings, more completed jobs and reliable behaviour lift someone up; poor behaviour pushes them down or off entirely.

03

The protection lives in the database

Filtering the map in the app is a convenience, not a safeguard — anything the app can ask for, anyone with the app’s public key can ask for. So worker phone numbers are not readable by the client, ID images sit outside any public bucket, and registration, bookings and admin actions all run through server-side functions.

04

Humans make the trust decisions

AI assists development, support triage and internal operations. Verification approvals, dispute outcomes, suspensions and anything touching money stay human-owned. Seva’s defensibility is trust and governance — automating away the human judgement that creates it would be self-defeating.

What is still being designed

A formal dispute-resolution flow, an appeals process for suspended workers, refund handling, and an incident-response runbook do not exist as systems yet — today they are handled directly by the founder. They are named here rather than glossed over, because a partner deserves to know which parts of the safety story are architecture and which parts are still a person answering the phone.

The booking

A job moves through states it cannot skip.

The customer books. The worker accepts, or the request is rejected. The worker does the job. An OTP confirms completion. Payment and rating follow. The transitions are explicit and checked — a booking cannot jump to “done” from anywhere.

→RequestedThe customer picks a worker and sends the request.
→AcceptedThe worker takes it, or declines and it ends there.
→In progressStarted by OTP, on site, with both people present.
→CompletedThe trust checkpoint. Nothing marks done without the code.
→PaidCash for the pilot. The amount is recorded against the booking.
→RatedFeedback is only possible on a job that actually finished.

Why the OTP matters more than it looks

It is the cheapest possible proof that a job happened. Without it, a completed booking is just a row someone typed — and every rating, every reputation and every future commission calculation rests on rows that mean nothing.

For the pilot the code lives inside the app rather than an SMS, because SMS costs money per message and proves nothing extra at this scale.

And why payment is still cash

Building an online payment stack before a single booking flow is proven would be an expensive way of avoiding the harder question. The app records the amount; the money moves the way it already moves in Varanasi.

That has one serious consequence for the business model, and we would rather state it than bury it — see the commission problem.

Pricing

Rate × hours, plus travel. That is the whole thing.

Complexity in pricing is almost always a transfer of value away from the person with less information. Three visible terms is the entire defence against that.

The formula

Total = rate × hours + travel

The worker chooses whether they charge hourly or daily, sets the number, and sets their own travel charge. A floor of ₹100 an hour and ₹300 a day is enforced at onboarding and in the admin console. There is no ceiling.

A decision worth explaining

We considered algorithmic surge, and chose not to.

A demand-driven price is sophisticated, and it silently moves price-setting from the worker to the platform — which breaks a non-negotiable. In a city where every customer talks to their neighbours, surge is also a reputational risk with no easy recovery.

The version we intend to build instead: when demand is high nearby, tell the worker so, and let them raise their rate if they want to. The algorithm advises. The worker decides.
